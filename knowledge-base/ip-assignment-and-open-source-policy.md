# Intellectual Property and Open Source Policy

Who owns what Meridian staff and contractors create, and which open source licenses
engineering teams may use.

## Ownership

- Work created by employees in the course of employment belongs to Meridian.
- Contractors and agencies must sign an IP assignment before starting work. Assignment on
  payment is not enough; it must assign on creation.
- Meridian never assigns its own IP to a customer or vendor without General Counsel approval
  (see [Signature Authority Matrix](signature-authority-matrix.md)). Customers get a licence instead.

## Open source licences

| Licence group | Examples | Use in internal tools | Use in software we distribute |
|---|---|---|---|
| Permissive | MIT, BSD-2/3, Apache-2.0, ISC | Allowed | Allowed, keep notices |
| Weak copyleft | LGPL-2.1, LGPL-3.0, MPL-2.0 | Allowed | Allowed if linked dynamically and unmodified; otherwise ask legal |
| Strong copyleft | GPL-2.0, GPL-3.0 | Allowed | Not allowed without IP counsel approval |
| Network copyleft | AGPL-3.0, SSPL | Ask IP counsel | Not allowed |
| No licence or unclear | Code copied from forums or blogs | Not allowed | Not allowed |

## Contributing to open source

Staff may contribute to open source projects on work time with their manager's approval,
if the contribution contains no Meridian confidential information and the project's
contributor agreement does not transfer Meridian patents. IP counsel reviews contributor
licence agreements.

## Third-party code in deliverables

Vendors must list every open source component in their deliverables with its licence.
Missing lists are a reason to reject the delivery.
